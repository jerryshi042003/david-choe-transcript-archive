Mike Cherman: How Bootlegging, Culture, and Creativity Built Chinatown Market

First Smoke of the Day

A two-hour retrospective on learning the streetwear business from the floor up, building through bootlegs and fast cultural response, then turning those instincts into teams, collaborations, stores, and MARKET.

What Tom should take from this

Mike repeatedly values proximity, making, and visible initiative over polished credentials. Tom should bring a finished sample and a useful production question—not a broad request to be mentored.

Interview details and stories

  1. 00:04:57

    Goodwood made the industry tangible

    Mike’s first New York role involved assembling wooden jewelry during the blog era. Starting with repetitive shop work put him near the products, artists, and information networks he wanted to understand.

  2. 00:12:35

    Learn business by standing near the work

    Mike describes watching deals, collections, collections of payment, and follow-through while working around experienced operators. Observation became useful because he was also doing the ordinary labor inside the room.

  3. 00:15:10

    Leadership includes admitting your own mistakes

    Looking back on an employee relationship, Mike separates the other person’s decision to leave from his own responsibility for how he managed people. The lesson is to learn without turning every departure into betrayal.

  4. 00:25:05

    Scenes change faster than brand mythology

    He compares early ComplexCon crowds in graphic T-shirts with later fashion cycles built around different music and styling. A brand cannot freeze the culture at the moment it first succeeded.

  5. 00:45:05

    Translate a place into product details

    A California shoe used In-N-Out line graphics, palms, pool color, and checkerboard references. The story shows how a collaboration becomes specific through many small observed details rather than one logo swap.

  6. 01:09:38

    A bootleg can become real cultural study

    The Grateful Dead relationship began with an unofficial idea, then deepened through licensed work and actual participation in the culture. Mike distinguishes borrowing an icon from taking the time to understand the people around it.

  7. 01:12:34

    The internet changed street culture

    He contrasts physical Fairfax lineups and block parties with culture now being produced for online display. The loss is not simply nostalgia; online distribution changes how people meet and how a scene proves it exists.

  8. 01:25:02

    Bootlegging has a boundary

    Mike distinguishes an interpretive bootleg from another company reproducing his finished product for profit. The difference is not a clean legal theory, but he recognizes that transformation and direct copying are not the same act.

  9. 01:35:00

    A brand can also be a production bridge

    He describes finding specialist makers, bringing them an idea and identity, and connecting their capability to another company. That agency role is different from merely placing MARKET graphics on an object.

  10. 01:44:52

    Former employees are part of the result

    The conversation returns to people who learned inside Mike’s companies and later carried those skills elsewhere. He treats that spread of capability as evidence of the organization’s value, even when relationships were imperfect.

  11. 01:59:32

    Start before approval

    His closing advice is to pursue the thing, tolerate judgment, and stop letting fear of failure prevent the first move. He recommends reaching out to people you respect because many of them will answer a sincere, concrete question.
