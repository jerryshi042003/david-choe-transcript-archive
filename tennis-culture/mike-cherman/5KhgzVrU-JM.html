Interview details and stories

  1. 00:04:46

    The first inventory was funded with personal savings

    As a teenager Mike used gifted savings to buy blank shirts. The decision turned an interest into inventory and forced him to learn what happened after the design.

  2. 00:09:59

    A lost sports identity created room for shirts

    When basketball receded, making became the practice Mike took seriously.

  3. 00:14:46

    The expected design education was not the real curriculum

    Mike entered a new Parsons program assuming it mixed design and business, then discovered it leaned differently. The mismatch pushed more learning into jobs and self-directed work.

  4. 00:24:51

    Fast execution created unexpected relationships

    Quick graphic work inside a customization shop led to designing early Kith material with Ronnie Fieg. Being useful in the moment mattered more than a formal introduction.

  5. 00:34:48

    Scaling wholesale exposed a product mismatch

    ICNY reached an appealing outdoor retailer, but expanding quickly revealed that the assortment did not fully serve the technical customer. Distribution amplified the unresolved product problem.

  6. 00:44:47

    Failure had shared causes

    When ICNY collapsed, Mike accepts his lack of experience while also describing advisers and partners who assigned blame. The cleaned lesson is neither total innocence nor total self-condemnation.

  7. 00:54:49

    Freelance success did not replace the desire to own a brand

    After ICNY, Mike could have continued earning through services, but he still wanted a new product platform. Chinatown Market emerged from that unresolved need to build.

  8. 01:00:02

    Streetwear purity can hide bad business reasoning

    He challenges the idea that broad distribution is automatically betrayal when another industry would celebrate the same demand.

  9. 01:05:13

    Refunding a hit clarified the opportunity

    A fast release infringed a trademark and every order had to be refunded. The painful event still proved that speed and cultural timing could work if redirected into defensible ideas.

  10. 01:12:20

    Invisible production relationships hold the work together

    Mike credits a trusted production partner as a cornerstone across multiple projects.

  11. 01:15:12

    Do not work ahead of the contract again

    After ICNY, excitement is no longer enough; he waits for signed terms and real comfort before committing labor.
